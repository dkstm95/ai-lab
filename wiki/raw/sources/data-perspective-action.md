## Ingest Focus

This is a paraphrased note of the Data-Perspective-Action framework and its
practical implications for turning trustworthy data into organizational
decisions.

## Structured Notes

- Reliable pipelines, schemas, and dashboards are necessary foundations, but
  their production does not prove that the organization makes better
  decisions.
- The framework separates three layers: Data provides trustworthy evidence;
  Perspective interprets what that evidence means in the current business
  context; Action turns the interpretation into a recommendation, decision,
  and follow-through.
- As AI makes pipeline, analysis, and dashboard production cheaper, governance,
  trusted definitions, domain knowledge, and judgment become more important.
- Data professionals can fall into an objectivity trap by delivering facts
  without an interpretation. The stakeholder then performs the hardest
  reasoning with less context and less analytical craft.
- A proposed habit is a weekly one-page narrative with three parts: what the
  data shows, what it means now, and what should happen next.
- The framework recommends a small set of agreed business health metrics and
  their explanatory input metrics, co-designing with stakeholders before the
  artifact is fully polished, and prohibiting data-only presentations.
- Recommendations should include opportunity sizing and continued advocacy.
  Teams should track which recommendations produced action and use the result
  as a learning agenda.
- One organizational pattern pairs an analytics engineer responsible for
  reliability with an analyst responsible for narrative, stakeholder
  relationships, and action advocacy inside one business domain.

## Source Limits

The framework is based primarily on the author's operating experience rather
than controlled comparative research. Reader comments identify an important
constraint: perspective work requires relational authority, business context,
and capacity, so a new habit alone cannot repair a role or organization that
does not grant access to decisions.
