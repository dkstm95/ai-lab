Authors: Vikram Sreekanti and Joseph E. Gonzalez
Published: 2026-07-09
Retrieved: 2026-07-27

## Ingest Focus

This is a paraphrased source note. It preserves the article's two-axis market
map and its distinction between high-volume feedback data and customer-specific
workflow knowledge.

## Structured Notes

- The article maps AI applications on two axes: difficulty of solving the
  problem and difficulty of adopting the product.
- Easy-to-adopt products can collect usage and feedback quickly. Coding agents
  are the central example because developers can adopt them individually and
  produce many accepted or rejected suggestions each day.
- Easy-to-solve and easy-to-adopt applications are described as a value trap:
  low entry barriers, large distribution advantages for frontier labs, and low
  user loyalty make displacement easy.
- Hard-to-solve but easy-to-adopt applications can build a fast data flywheel,
  but they also attract well-capitalized model providers and can remain weakly
  sticky when users can switch tools.
- Easy-to-solve but hard-to-adopt enterprise products gain defensibility from
  integrations and customer-specific operating knowledge. The data may not be
  reusable for model training across customers, but the learned configuration
  can make replacement costly.
- Hard-to-solve and hard-to-adopt products, such as SRE and security
  operations, may acquire the most valuable workflow knowledge. Their learning
  loop is slower because events are less frequent, implementations are custom,
  and correct outcomes are harder to verify.
- User experience can move a product between adoption quadrants. Reducing
  adoption friction is therefore also a data acquisition strategy.

## Source Limits

"Data is your only moat" is a deliberately strong headline. The article itself
also relies on integration depth, capital, distribution, user experience, and
switching costs, so its operational argument is better read as "unique learning
data is necessary" than "all other advantages are irrelevant." The authors
disclose an investment in a company positioned in the hard-to-adopt,
hard-to-solve quadrant.
