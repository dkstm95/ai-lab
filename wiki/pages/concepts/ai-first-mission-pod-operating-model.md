## Summary

An AI-first mission pod is a small cross-functional team responsible for one outcome and the complete learning loop needed to improve it, with explicit assignments for people, AI, and human review.

## Operating Loop

1. Define one customer, product, or revenue outcome.
2. Gather direct customer evidence and set a release-quality bar.
3. Map each task to a person, AI, or AI with human review.
4. Prototype, release, measure the outcome, and inspect failures.
5. Change the product, process, responsibility map, or team from what the result shows.

## Responsibility Map

| Assignment | Use when | Required control |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Human | The work needs judgment, customer trust, accountability, or expertise that AI cannot meet | Name the accountable decision-maker and success measure |
| AI | The result is bounded, testable, and safe enough to automate | Record the input, output, and evaluation |
| AI with human review | AI can create useful work but the release risk still needs judgment | Define the reviewer, checks, and escalation path |

- accepted: Outcome ownership removes departmental handoffs from the pod's customer-to-iteration loop.

- accepted: Cross-functional pod members need strong communication because individual AI-assisted speed does not coordinate the team by itself.

- accepted: Explicit human, AI, and review assignments make automation boundaries visible and testable.

## Application Notes

- hypothesis: Start with one pod and one measurable outcome before changing the whole organization.
- hypothesis: Review the responsibility map after each release because model quality, risk, and team skills change over time.
- hypothesis: Track time to measured learning, customer outcome change, escaped defects, and repeated failures before using output per person as a success measure.
- hypothesis: Approve new headcount when the pod can show an outcome gap that remains after safe automation and clearer responsibility.
